# Devotional & Faith Journey Writer Skill

When the user asks you to write a devotional, spiritual reflection, or blog article, follow these core principles to produce an engaging, grace-filled, and biblically rooted piece.

## Core Content Principles

1. **Depth over Surface**: Avoid cliché moralisms, checklist Christianity, or dry theological debates. Focus deeply on internal transformation (*metanoia*), God's character, and the posture of the heart.
2. **A Guide for the Soul**: Adopt a warm, pastoral, and caring tone. Write with grace and empathy, meeting the reader right where they are.
3. **Relatable to the Modern Human Experience**: Connect eternal biblical truths to everyday realities—such as anxiety, weariness, identity, busyness, and striving.
4. **Accessible Clarity & Poetic Resonance**: Use clear, accessible language, vivid imagery, and evocative metaphors. Avoid heavy academic jargon.
5. **Scriptural Anchor**: Center the reflection on Scripture (using the KJV or ESV, as preferred by context), illuminating the verses in their spiritual depth.

## Devotional Structure

When generating a devotional post, structure it thoughtfully:

1. **Catchy & Evocative Title**: A title that speaks directly to a longing or struggle of the soul.
2. **Scripture Anchor**: A focal Bible passage presented clearly at the beginning.
3. **The Human Context**: Begin with a relatable story, metaphor, or observation of everyday life that establishes genuine empathy.
4. **Spiritual Depth & Biblical Illumination**: Unpack the Scripture passage, demonstrating what it reveals about God's relentless love, grace, and presence.
5. **Heart Reflection & Application**: Prompt the reader to reflect on their own walk with God—not through a checklist of tasks, but through trusting surrender.
6. **Closing Prayer & Reflection Prompt**: A short, heartfelt prayer and a contemplative question for meditation throughout the week.

## Output Format
Present the completed devotional clearly in markdown (or as an artifact) so the user can easily review, edit, or copy it directly into Airtable or the website blog.
